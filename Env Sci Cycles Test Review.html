⚠️ Easy-to-mix-up stuff

  • Nitrogen in the air is N₂ (two nitrogens, triple bond). Not NO₂.
  • Plants get nitrogen as nitrates (NO₃⁻) from the soil, made by bacteria, lightning or fertilizer. They get phosphorus as phosphates from the soil, released when rock weathers.
  • Decomposers send carbon to the air (as CO₂), but send nitrogen (ammonium) and phosphorus (phosphates) back to the soil and water.
  • Fertilizer runoff changes both the nitrogen and phosphorus cycles in a pond. Fertilizer has nitrates and phosphates → algae bloom.
  • Cellular respiration is not breathing. It's a reaction inside cells, and plants do it too.
  • Human carbon = fossil fuels + cutting forests = 9.8 + 1.4 = 11.2. The air keeps 5.6 of it, so about half stays in the atmosphere.
  • Carbon's biomolecules: all four (carbs, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids). Nitrogen: proteins (amino acids) + DNA/RNA. Phosphorus: DNA/RNA, phospholipids, bones.

🌐 Earth's four spheres

☁️ Atmosphereair
🌿 Biosphereliving things
🌊 Hydrospherewater, especially the ocean
🪨 Geosphererocks, soil, fossil fuels

Atoms are never created or destroyed, only moved and rearranged (conservation of matter). That's why there was a C or an N in every row of the game.

Carbon path

☁️ CO₂ in air → photosynthesis → 🌿 plants → eating → 🐄 animals → respiration / decomposition → ☁️ air
Slow side: ☁️ air → dissolves (esp. cold water) → 🌊 ocean → shells of CaCO₃ → 🪨 limestone → volcanoes / erosion → back
Buried remains + heat + pressure → 🛢️ fossil fuels → combustion (humans) → ☁️ air

Rocks like limestone hold the most carbon overall. The ocean holds the most active carbon (38,000 Gt vs 900 in air ≈ 42×).

Nitrogen path

☁️ N₂ gas → nitrogen fixation (bacteria in soil & root nodules of beans/peas, lightning, factories) → NH₄⁺ ammonium → nitrification (soil bacteria) → NO₃⁻ nitrates → assimilation (roots) → 🌿 plants → eating → 🐇 animals → waste/death → ammonification (decomposers: fungi & bacteria) → NH₄⁺
Nitrates → denitrification (denitrifying bacteria) → ☁️ N₂ gas

Phosphorus path

🪨 sedimentary rock → weathering → PO₄³⁻ phosphates in soil → roots → 🌿 plants → eating → 🐭 animals → decomposers → soil & water
Soil/fertilizer → surface runoff → 🌊 lakes & ocean → algae → marine food chain → remains settle → sedimentation → 🪨 new rock → uplift (millions of yrs) → land

Phosphorus is often a limiting nutrient: it's in short supply, so it limits how much living things can grow (especially in lakes & ponds).
